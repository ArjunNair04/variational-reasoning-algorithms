# Q5 early-versus-late prior weighting: completed results

## Main finding

Reducing the prior weight on the second half of each reasoning trace is the
most promising setting in this screen. It reached **81.25% Final Acc@1**, up
from **80.17%** for the same-seed historical Q5 control. Strict final accuracy
also increased, from **69.25% to 73.08%**. Its improvement over the control is
not yet resolved: the descriptive interval for the final gain is
**-1.50 to +3.50 percentage points**.

The clearer comparison is between the two positional changes. Reducing the
second-half weight beat reducing the first-half weight on all three seeds,
both for extracted and strict final accuracy. Reducing both halves uniformly
left mean final accuracy unchanged. These results suggest that *where* we
reduce the prior contribution may matter, rather than simply reducing its
overall strength.

## What changed

The experiment changes how candidate traces are weighted before training.
Each reasoning trace is divided at its token midpoint. The model still sees
the complete trace and is trained on the same complete reasoning-and-answer
target; no tokens are removed from the training loss.

For early and late reasoning log probabilities H and T, answer log probability
A, and unchanged answer-marker log probability M, the candidate score is:

    score = A + M + alpha * H + beta * T
    responsibility = softmax(score across the question's buffer)

The known answer and end-of-sequence token are unchanged. The proposal remains
answer-derived, the reader remains moving, and the buffer remains a persistent
unique FIFO buffer of 16 traces. All trained cells use 32 rounds, 2,048 training
generations, 32 optimizer steps and attention-plus-MLP rank-16 LoRA.

This is a positional split, not a verified separation between understanding
the question and calculating its answer. The earlier trace audit found that
calculation often begins on the first line.

## Accuracy

All values are means across seeds **1201, 1213 and 1217**. The frozen base and
standard Q5 are the original same-seed controls, not newly trained cells.
Evaluation uses the same 400 training-derived validation questions.

| Setting | Early / late weight | Final Acc@1 | Strict final | Acc@1 AUC |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Frozen base | Not trained | 75.50% | 61.50% | 75.50% |
| Standard Q5 | 1 / 1 | 80.17% | 69.25% | 79.30% |
| Reduce first half | 0.5 / 1 | 80.17% | 67.17% | **79.82%** |
| Reduce second half | 1 / 0.5 | **81.25%** | **73.08%** | 79.58% |
| Reduce both uniformly | 0.75 / 0.75 | 80.17% | 69.75% | 79.32% |

Final Acc@1 uses the extracted numerical answer. Strict final additionally
requires the declared answer format. AUC is the trapezoidal average of
checkpoint accuracy over rounds 0-32, including the seed's frozen baseline.
Strict AUC, full checkpoint records and all per-seed metrics are retained in
the accompanying analysis files.

differences are a reason for confirmation rather than a final superiority claim.

## Learning over time

| Setting | Round 0 | Round 8 | Round 16 | Round 24 | Round 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard Q5 | 75.50% | 76.83% | 81.33% | 81.33% | 80.17% |
| Reduce first half | 75.50% | 78.25% | 81.75% | 82.08% | 80.17% |
| Reduce second half | 75.50% | 77.92% | 80.83% | 81.67% | 81.25% |
| Reduce both uniformly | 75.50% | 77.17% | 81.58% | 81.17% | 80.17% |

First-half attenuation learns faster but gives up its advantage at the end.
Second-half attenuation has a smaller decline from its best mean checkpoint:
0.42 points, compared with 1.17 for standard Q5 and 1.92 for first-half
attenuation. Its endpoint advantage therefore does not arise from having
the best average accuracy throughout training.

## What the buffer diagnostics show

The following means cover the logged nonempty question-round supports, not
the entire buffer population. Support counts are 382, 381 and 382 respectively.

| Setting | Mean candidates | Effective candidates (ESS) | Largest weight | Weight-length rank correlation | Top trace changed vs joint score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reduce first half | 7.90 | 1.21 | 91.79% | -0.410 | 11.52% |
| Reduce second half | 7.95 | 1.15 | 93.76% | -0.417 | 10.76% |
| Reduce both uniformly | 7.97 | 1.16 | 93.63% | -0.384 | 2.88% |

The intervention changes which trace receives the largest weight, but does
not remove concentration on one trace. The second-half setting performs best
at the endpoint despite having a more concentrated distribution than the
first-half setting. Better accuracy here is therefore not explained by a
simple increase in effective buffer size.

Against uniform scoring on the same observed support, the preferred trace
changes on 11.52% of first-half supports and 9.97% of second-half supports.
These are counterfactual scores on each trained cell's own evolving support;
they do not compare identical buffers across different training trajectories.

Strict format failures fall from 19.42% in standard Q5 to 15.42% with
second-half attenuation; first-half attenuation instead reaches 22.33%.
Natural EOS rates are 93.75%, 94.33% and 93.42%, respectively. No final
evaluation contains multiple answer markers. The larger strict-accuracy gain
is consistent with improved output compliance, alongside the smaller gain
in extracted correctness.

## Cost

Means per seed; GPU hours include evaluation on one NVIDIA H100 80GB.
Backward tokens count target-token positions in backward passes, including
repeated use, rather than unique generated tokens.

| Setting | Training generations | Optimizer steps | Backward tokens | GPU hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard Q5 | 2,048 | 32 | 156,333 | 0.577 |
| Reduce first half | 2,048 | 32 | 157,100 | 0.641 |
| Reduce second half | 2,048 | 32 | 159,602 | 0.668 |
| Reduce both uniformly | 2,048 | 32 | 159,116 | 0.669 |

The second-half setting uses the same generation and update budgets, about
2.1% more backward tokens and 15.8% more recorded GPU time than the historical
control. The positional diagnostics add two scoring forwards, and execution
timing is not a contemporaneously matched benchmark.

## Decision

The unchanged selection rule nominates **Q5-LATE-HALF**: it improves mean final
extracted accuracy without lowering mean strict final accuracy. AUC is not
a veto under this protocol. Neither first-half nor uniform attenuation improves
mean final accuracy over the control.

Keep standard Q5 as the established setting. The next useful decision is
whether to confirm second-half attenuation on fresh paired seeds against
standard Q5 and the uniform control. This would test whether the positional
advantage survives new training randomness. No additional runs were submitted.
